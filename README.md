## The number after the name is width in centimetres

Before anything else, one piece of vocabulary. Coach style names come with a trailing number — Tabby 26, Brooklyn 34, Chelsea 36 — and that number is essentially the bag's width in centimetres. Bigger number, bigger bag. It's not a version number and it's not a size rank within the family.

That single detail resolves most of the "28 or 34?" arguments you'll find in comment sections. A 158 cm shopper trying a Brooklyn 28 and a Brooklyn 34 is looking at two genuinely different categories of bag, not two sizes of the same one. The smaller one reads as a shoulder bag; the larger one reads as a work bag.

Tabby spans the widest range of any Coach family right now — 12, 19, 20, 26, 30 and 36 have all appeared in the range, across glove-tanned leather, metallic finishes, quilted sheep leather, suede and recycled denim. Brooklyn comes in 23, 28, 34 and 39. Empire sits around 34. Chelsea runs 30 and 36.

## Material choice outlasts colour choice

The most expensive mistake in this category is picking by colourway. The material is what determines whether the bag still looks good in two years.

Grain leather is the safe default. It takes scuffs with grace, softens with use, and hides the marks that daily commuting produces. Suede looks better in photographs but scratches don't heal, and a scratched suede Brooklyn is not a repair you can easily undo. Quilted sheep leather and smooth lambskin photograph beautifully but stain, particularly the pale colourways, where dark denim and dark coats transfer pigment over time.

If you carry the bag on a crowded train five days a week, buy the material for the commute and the colour for the outfit. If it's an occasional bag, the fragile options are genuinely fine.

## Where this actually breaks

Honesty about the failure modes is more useful than a longer sales pitch.

**Returns kill cashback.** If you send the bag back, the commission reverses and so does the cashback. Worth knowing if you're ordering two sizes to try at home.

**Gift cards usually replace cashback rather than stack with it.** Gift cards sit outside commission structures, and most merchants exclude both buying them and paying with them. Discounted gift cards can beat a cashback rate outright — do the comparison rather than assuming both apply.

**Tracking breaks quietly.** A detour to another coupon site, a comparison tab, or a coupon extension popping up at payment can reassign the referral. If it happens, reactivate through ShopBack immediately before completing the order.

**Jewellery, fine leather and some brand categories carry exclusions at certain merchants.** Read the store page terms before you build the purchase around a rate. And note that rates and exclusions differ between the app and the web for the same merchant, which is why the live page — not a blog post, including this one — is the number to check.
